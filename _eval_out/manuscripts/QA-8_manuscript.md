## Answer (EN)

The paper defines an inventory of 18 propagandist techniques (arXiv:1910.09982):

1. **Loaded language** — words/phrases with strong emotional implications to influence the audience
2. **Name calling or labeling** — labeling the object as something the audience fears, hates, or praises
3. **Repetition** — repeating the same message so the audience eventually accepts it
4. **Exaggeration or minimization** — presenting something as larger/better/worse or less important than it is
5. **Doubt** — questioning the credibility of someone or something
6. **Appeal to fear/prejudice** — instilling anxiety/panic toward an alternative, possibly based on preconceived judgments
7. **Flag-waving** — playing on strong national or group feeling to justify an action or idea
8. **Causal oversimplification** — assuming one cause (incl. scapegoating) where multiple exist
9. **Slogans** — brief, striking phrases acting as emotional appeals
10. **Appeal to authority** — claiming something is true because an authority/expert supports it
11. **Black-and-white fallacy, dictatorship** — presenting two options as the only possibilities
12. **Thought-terminating cliché** — short, generic phrases discouraging critical thought
13. **Whataboutism** — charging the opponent with hypocrisy without disproving their argument
14. **Reductio ad Hitlerum** — disapproval by associating an idea with hated groups
15. **Red herring** — introducing irrelevant material to divert attention
16. **Bandwagon** — persuading because "everyone else is taking the same action"
17. **Obfuscation, intentional vagueness, confusion** — deliberately unclear words
18. **Straw man** — substituting the opponent's proposition with a similar one and refuting it
